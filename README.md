# Vast Ores and Vast Depths

Two content mods for [Horizon](https://playhorizon.dev): modded ores, metals and end-game materials, with no code to build.

## Vast Ores (`vastores`)

- **Ten metals:** aluminum, lead, nickel, osmium, platinum, silver, tin, uranium, zinc and iridium. Each has a stone and a
  deepslate ore, raw ore, raw block, ingot, nugget, block, dust, plate, gear and rod.
- **Three gems:** ruby, sapphire and peridot, with ore, gem, block and dust.
- **Nine alloys:** bronze, brass, constantan, electrum, invar, steel, enderium, lumium and signalum. Mix their dusts in
  the crafting grid, then smelt the alloy dust.
- Dust, plate, gear and rod for iron, gold and copper too.
- **Ore hammer** (three iron ingots, two sticks): hammer + raw ore = two dusts, hammer + ingot = a plate. It isn't used up.
- Ores generate in the overworld, with a second, richer layer below y 0 for players who dig deep.
- `c:` convention tags (`c:ingots/tin`, `c:dusts/bronze`...) for other mods' recipes.

## Vast Depths (`vastdepths`, needs Vast Ores)

| Metal | Where | Mined with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stellarite | deep overworld, y −64 to −32 | netherite |
| Infernium | the Nether, y 8 to 40 | stellarite |
| Voidium | the End, in end stone | infernium |

Each has the full set of parts, plus a pickaxe, axe, shovel and sword (ingots and blaze rods). Three alloys take one ingot
of each metal and a netherite ingot.

## Ore generation

The numbers are in `config.json`: for each material, a list of veins with `count` (veins per chunk, 0 turns one off),
`size`, `min_y`, `max_y` and `shape` (`trapezoid` or `uniform`). Players who want other numbers can unzip the `.hmod`
into a folder in `horizon-mods/` and edit its `data/<mod>/worldgen/` files; only new chunks change.

## Building

```sh
./release.sh   # builds both mods from tools/build_content.py and config.json, packs dist/*.hmod
./apply.sh     # builds and installs them into your own Horizon (macOS paths)
```

Everything is generated by `tools/build_content.py`. The textures are drawn pixel by pixel as grids in `tools/art.py`,
coloured from a ramp per material; a finished PNG in `art/` replaces any of them (see `art/README.md`), and
`python3 tools/preview.py build sheet.png` lays every texture out on one sheet.
